What is compound interest? (and why time matters most)

It's been called the most powerful force in finance — and once it clicks, a lot of money advice suddenly makes sense. Here's the simple version.

In one line

Earning returns on your returns

Compound interest means you earn returns not just on your original money, but also on the returns it already earned. Over time, the growth feeds on itself and snowballs.

Why time is the real lever

The longer money compounds, the more dramatic the snowball. That's why the biggest factor often isn't how much you start with — it's how early you start. Even small amounts, given enough time, can outgrow larger amounts started late.

A rough rule of thumb

The "Rule of 72": divide 72 by an annual rate to roughly estimate the years it takes money to double. At 7% a year, that's about 10 years — a quick way to feel how compounding accelerates. (It's an estimate, not a promise, and real returns vary and aren't certain.)

It cuts both ways

Compounding also works against you with high-interest debt — the balance can snowball just as fast. Which is exactly why clearing high-interest debt early is so powerful.

Curious about crypto?

Foundations harness time; hype fights it

Compounding rewards patience and a long horizon — the opposite of chasing quick crypto gains. If you're curious about crypto, learn it carefully as a small, high-risk slice, only with money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

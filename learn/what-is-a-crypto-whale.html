What is a crypto whale? (and why they matter)

You'll see "whale alert" posts everywhere. Here's who whales are, why their moves matter, and why chasing them is a trap.

In one line

A holder big enough to move the price

A whale is someone — a person or an entity — holding a very large amount of a coin. Enough that their buying or selling can noticeably move the price, especially in smaller markets.

Why they matter

In a thin market, one big whale order can swing the price sharply, which is why low-liquidity coins are extra volatile. Whale movements also get watched and hyped, sometimes triggering everyone else to react.

Why following them is risky

It's tempting to "follow the smart money," but you rarely know a whale's full intent or timing. They might be hedging, moving between wallets, or setting a trap. Copying blindly can leave you buying the top. Treat whale activity as context, not a signal — and stick to your own research.

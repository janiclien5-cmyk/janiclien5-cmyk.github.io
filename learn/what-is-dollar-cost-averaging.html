What is dollar-cost averaging (DCA)?

DCA is the beginner-friendly strategy that takes the agony of "is now the right time?" off the table. Here's how it works — and what it does and doesn't do.

In one line

Invest a fixed amount on a schedule

Dollar-cost averaging means investing the same amount at regular intervals — say weekly or monthly — no matter the price. You spread your buying over time instead of trying to pick the perfect moment.

Why beginners like it

  • No timing stress: you don't have to guess the top or bottom.
  • Less emotion: a schedule beats reacting to hype or fear.
  • Averages your price: you buy more when it's low, less when it's high.

The honest limits

DCA does not remove risk — the asset can still fall, and you can still lose money, especially with something as volatile as crypto. It's a discipline tool that smooths out timing, not a way to promise gains. And remember: only invest money you can afford to lose.

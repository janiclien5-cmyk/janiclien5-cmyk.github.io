How does bitcoin work? (a beginner's explainer)

You don't need to be technical to get the gist. Here's bitcoin in plain English — what it is, how it keeps running, and why it moves around so much.

In one line

Digital money on a shared public ledger

Bitcoin is money recorded on a blockchain — a shared, public record that a worldwide network keeps in sync. No single bank or company is in charge of it.

How the network keeps it honest

Thousands of computers worldwide hold a copy of the ledger and check each other's work, so no one can quietly fake a transaction. "Miners" bundle transactions into blocks and compete to add them, earning newly issued bitcoin for the effort — which is also how new coins enter circulation.

The fixed supply

Only 21 million bitcoin will ever exist, and the rate of new issuance halves roughly every four years (the "halving"). That built-in scarcity is a core part of the idea — though scarcity alone doesn't set the price.

Why it's so volatile

Bitcoin is still a relatively young, sentiment-driven market that trades 24/7 with no daily close. Big news can move it fast in either direction. That's why beginners are wiser to start small and learn first than to chase price moves.

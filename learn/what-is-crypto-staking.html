What is crypto staking? (a beginner's explainer)

You'll see "earn rewards by staking" everywhere. Here's what it actually means, where the rewards come from, and the risks the hype usually skips.

In one line

Lock up coins to help run a network — and maybe earn rewards

Many crypto networks use "proof of stake": people lock up coins to help secure the network, and in return can receive rewards. Think of it as being paid for helping keep the system running — not as free money.

Where the rewards come from

Rewards are mostly new coins the network issues plus a share of transaction fees, paid to those who stake. The rate varies by network and over time, and it's quoted before the coin's own price moves — which can easily swing more than the reward.

The risks the hype skips

  • Price risk. Your staked coin can fall in value more than you earn.
  • Lockups. Funds may be locked or take days to unstake — you can't always sell fast.
  • Changing rewards. The reward rate isn't fixed or promised.
  • Slashing. Some networks penalize validator faults, which can reduce a stake.

The beginner takeaway

Staking is worth understanding, not rushing into. Learn how a specific network's staking works, check the lockup terms, and never stake money you can't afford to have locked up or lose. See the glossary for the terms.

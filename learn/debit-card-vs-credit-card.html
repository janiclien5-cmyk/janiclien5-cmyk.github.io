Debit card vs credit card: what's the difference?

They look the same and tap the same — but they work very differently under the hood. Knowing which is which helps you avoid expensive mistakes.

The core difference

Your money vs borrowed money

A debit card spends money you already have in your account. A credit card borrows up to a limit that you repay later — with interest if you don't pay in full.

How they compare

  • Overspending: a debit card can't (you only spend what's there); a credit card can.
  • Debt risk: debit has none; credit can pile up interest if mismanaged.
  • Building credit: a credit card, used well, can build your credit score; debit usually doesn't.
  • Protections: credit cards often offer stronger fraud/purchase protections (varies by country).

The safe way to use credit

Treat a credit card like a debit card: only spend what you already have, and pay the full balance every month. Then you get the upsides (building credit, protections) without the interest trap.

Curious about crypto?

Spend within your means first

The same discipline that keeps a credit card safe — only spend what you actually have — applies to crypto too. Get the everyday basics and your savings in place first; then, if you're curious, learn about crypto carefully, only with money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

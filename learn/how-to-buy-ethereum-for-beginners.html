How to buy Ethereum (ETH) for beginners

No hype, no promises — just the sensible order to buy your first ETH, with the basics you should understand before you do.

Before you buy

Know what you're buying, and only risk spare money

Ethereum is a programmable blockchain; ETH is its coin, used to pay network ("gas") fees. Prices are volatile and on-chain mistakes usually can't be reversed — so learn first, go slow, and never use money you actually need.

Step 1 — Understand ETH (briefly)

Unlike bitcoin (designed mainly as digital money), Ethereum runs apps and contracts, and ETH powers them. You don't need to be an expert — just know what it is and that few coins endure, so curiosity beats chasing hype.

Step 2 — Pick a reputable exchange

Most beginners buy ETH on a well-known exchange. Availability depends on your country, so check your local eligibility first, and compare fees and security.

Step 3 — Verify and secure your account

  • Expect identity verification (KYC) — it's standard and required by law.
  • Turn on app-based 2FA and use a unique, strong password.
  • Never share your seed phrase, and verify every link and address.

Step 4 — Buy a small amount

You don't need a whole ETH — you can buy a fraction. Start small while you learn; a market order buys at the current price, a limit order only fills at a price you set.

Step 5 — Store it safely

Crypto left on an exchange is custodied by the platform; moving it to a wallet you control gives you the keys and the full responsibility. Learn the trade-off before holding larger amounts.

What is a blockchain? (explained simply)

It's the word behind all of crypto — and it's simpler than it sounds. Here's the plain-English version, with the analogy that makes it click.

In one line

A shared record that's very hard to fake

A blockchain is a digital record of transactions, copied across many computers, with entries grouped into "blocks" that link together in order. Because everyone holds a copy, no one can quietly rewrite history.

The analogy that makes it click

Imagine a shared notebook that thousands of people each hold an identical copy of. Every time something happens, everyone writes the same new line. To fake an entry, you'd have to change it in most of the notebooks at the exact same time — practically impossible. That's the core idea.

Why "chain" of "blocks"

Transactions are bundled into blocks, and each block references the one before it — forming a chain. Tampering with an old block would break every block after it, which the network would instantly notice.

What it's good (and not good) for

  • Good for: records that need to be shared, tamper-resistant, and not controlled by one party.
  • Not magic: it doesn't make data "true," remove all risk, or prove a project is legit.

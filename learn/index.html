First, the honest part

Crypto can lose value — fast

Prices swing hard, scams are common, and on-chain mistakes usually can't be undone. That's not a reason to avoid learning — it's the reason to learn before you act. Only ever explore with money you can afford to lose.

3 things to understand first

  • A wallet is not a bank. You hold the keys; there's no "forgot password" and no one to reverse a bad transfer.
  • An exchange is where you buy, sell, and learn the ropes. It handles the buying/selling; you still set a strong password and turn on 2FA.
  • Most coins won't last. Thousands exist; few endure. Curiosity is good — chasing hype is how beginners get hurt.

The safety rules that matter most

  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) — app-based, not SMS.
  • Never share your seed phrase. No real support team will ask for it.
  • Check every link and address before you click or send. Verify, then act.
  • Ignore DMs promising easy or "sure" profits. If it sounds too good, it is.
  • Start small. There's no prize for rushing.

Beginner questions, answered honestly

Is crypto safe for beginners?
It carries real risk — prices are volatile, you can lose money, and most on-chain mistakes can't be undone. You lower the risk by learning first, turning on 2FA, never sharing your seed phrase, and only using money you can afford to lose.

How much money do I need to start?
There's no minimum worth rushing for. Most beginners start small on purpose while they learn how buying, selling, and securing crypto actually works.

Do I need a wallet or an exchange first?
Most people start on an exchange (where buying and selling happen), then learn about self-custody wallets as they go. A wallet holds your own keys — no password reset — so it's worth understanding before you rely on one.

What's the safest way to start?
Learn the basics, use a well-known exchange with strong security settings, enable app-based 2FA, verify every link and address, ignore messages promising easy profits, and start small.

Gross vs net pay: understanding your payslip

Ever wondered why your salary and your bank deposit don't match? That's gross vs net — and knowing the difference is the start of a budget that actually works.

The core difference

What you earn vs what you take home

Gross pay is your total earnings before anything's taken out. Net pay — your "take-home pay" — is what actually lands in your account after deductions. Net is the number that matters for budgeting.

What's deducted in between

  • Taxes — income tax and similar, depending on your country.
  • Other withholdings — things like pension/retirement contributions or insurance.

The exact items vary by country and job, but the idea is the same everywhere: gross minus deductions = net.

Why it matters

Always budget off your net pay, not your gross. Planning around the bigger gross number is a classic way to come up short every month.

Curious about crypto?

It comes out of net pay, after the basics

Anything you'd put toward learning about crypto comes from your net pay, well after your needs and savings are covered — never the rent money. Build those basics first; then, if you're curious, learn about crypto carefully, only with money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

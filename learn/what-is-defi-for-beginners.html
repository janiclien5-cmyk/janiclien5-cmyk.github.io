What is DeFi? (a beginner's explainer)

"DeFi" gets thrown around a lot. Here's what it actually means, what it's used for, and the risks that make it an advanced topic — not a beginner playground.

In one line

Financial services run by code, not a company

DeFi (decentralized finance) means things like lending, borrowing, or trading handled by smart contracts on a blockchain, instead of by a bank. You usually interact straight from your own wallet.

Common building blocks

  • Exchanges (DEXs) — swap tokens directly, wallet to wallet.
  • Lending/borrowing — earn on deposits or borrow against collateral.
  • Stablecoins — coins meant to track a steady value, used widely in DeFi.

The risks beginners must know

  • Smart-contract bugs. Code can have flaws that get exploited — funds can vanish.
  • Scams & fake apps. Anyone can launch a project or a lookalike site.
  • No safety net. No support line, no refunds, no reversing a mistake.
  • Complexity. More moving parts means more ways to lose money.

The beginner takeaway

DeFi is genuinely interesting, but it's an advanced area. Get comfortable with the basics, security, and a regular exchange first. See the glossary and how to avoid scams.

What is a satoshi? (you don't need a whole bitcoin)

One of the biggest beginner myths is that you need to buy a whole bitcoin. You don't — and the satoshi is why.

In one line

The smallest unit of bitcoin

A satoshi (or "sat") is one hundred-millionth of a single bitcoin — the smallest piece it divides into. It's named after Bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto.

Why it matters for beginners

Because bitcoin splits into 100 million sats, you can buy a tiny fraction — a few dollars' worth — rather than a whole coin. That removes the biggest "I can't afford it" barrier: you can start small and learn, exactly as the sensible approach suggests.

"Stacking sats"

You'll hear people talk about "stacking sats" — simply accumulating small amounts over time, often via dollar-cost averaging. Like any approach, it doesn't remove risk; only ever use money you can afford to lose.

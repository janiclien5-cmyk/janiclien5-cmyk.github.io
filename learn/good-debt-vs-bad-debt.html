Good debt vs bad debt: what's the difference?

Not all debt is equal. Some can build your future; some quietly drains it. Knowing which is which makes borrowing decisions far clearer.

The simple test

Does it build, or just drain?

Good debt tends to fund something that can grow your income or net worth over time, often at a lower rate. Bad debt funds things that lose value or just cover overspending, usually at high interest.

Rough examples

  • Often "good": sensible borrowing for education or a home — investments in the future.
  • Often "bad": high-interest credit-card balances for everyday spending or things that depreciate.

The line blurs — too much "good" debt is still risky, and the interest rate matters a lot.

The one question to ask

Before borrowing: "Is this likely to leave me better off, or just cost me?" If it's draining and high-interest, treat it as something to clear fast.

Curious about crypto?

Never borrow to buy crypto

Borrowing money to put into something as volatile as crypto is among the riskiest things a beginner can do — it can turn a dip into a disaster. Clear bad debt first; if you're curious about crypto, use only money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

How to improve your credit score (beginner basics)

A good credit score quietly saves you money on loans, cards, and rentals. The good news: the habits that build it are simple and within your control.

In one line

A number lenders use to judge risk

Your credit score is a snapshot of how reliably you've handled borrowed money. A higher score usually means easier approval and lower interest rates — which is real money saved.

What actually moves it

  • Payment history: paying on time is usually the single biggest factor.
  • Credit usage: using a low share of your available credit helps a lot.
  • Account age: a longer history generally helps.
  • New applications: lots of new accounts at once can ding it.

Simple habits that build it

  • Pay every bill on time — set up autopay for at least the minimums.
  • Keep card balances low relative to their limits.
  • Don't open lots of new accounts in a short span.
  • Check your credit report for errors and dispute them.

Improvement is gradual — consistency over time is what works, not any quick trick.

How to sign up for a crypto exchange (a beginner's step-by-step)

No hype, no promises — just what to expect when you create your first account, so the process feels simple instead of intimidating.

What you'll need

A few minutes, an email, and a photo ID

Most exchanges ask for an email, a strong password, and a government ID for identity verification. Set aside a few quiet minutes — and only ever plan to use money you can afford to lose once you're set up.

Step 1 — Pick a reputable exchange

Start on a well-known exchange to learn how buying, selling, and securing crypto actually works. Availability depends on your country, so check your local eligibility first.

Step 2 — Create your account

Sign up with your email and set a unique, strong password you don't use anywhere else. Bookmark the real site and type the address yourself — fake look-alike pages are a common way beginners get caught.

Step 3 — Verify your identity (KYC)

Regulated exchanges ask for ID by law — this is know-your-customer (KYC). Have a government ID ready and expect a short review. It's normal: your ID confirms you're a real person and meets anti-fraud rules — the same checks a bank does. An exchange asking for ID is the reassuring sign; one that lets you trade with no verification at all is the worrying one.

Step 4 — Turn on security before you fund it

  • Enable app-based 2FA (not SMS).
  • Never share your seed phrase or login codes — no real support team asks.
  • Verify every link and address before you click or send.

Step 5 — You're ready: start small

Once verified and secured, you can take a small first step while you learn. There's no prize for rushing — understanding, not hype, should guide what you do next.

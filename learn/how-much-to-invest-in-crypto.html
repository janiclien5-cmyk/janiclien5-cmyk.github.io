How much should a beginner invest in crypto?

There's no magic number — but there is a sensible way to think about it that keeps you out of the trouble most beginners get into.

The only rule that always holds

Only what you can afford to lose

Crypto can fall hard and fast. If losing the amount would change how you eat, sleep, or pay rent, it's too much. Full stop.

A simple order of priorities

  • Cover your essentials first — rent, bills, food, minimum debt payments.
  • Build an emergency fund before investing in anything volatile.
  • Pay down high-interest debt — it often "returns" more than any investment.
  • Then, only spare money you genuinely won't need goes toward learning.

Start smaller than you think

There's no prize for going big early. A small amount is enough to learn how buying, fees, security, and volatility actually feel — which is the real point at the start. You can always do more later, with more understanding.

Lump sum or a little at a time?

Many beginners prefer a fixed small amount on a schedule (dollar-cost averaging) rather than all at once, because it removes the stress of timing. Neither approach removes risk.

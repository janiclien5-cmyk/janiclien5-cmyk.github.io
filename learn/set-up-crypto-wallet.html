How to set up a crypto wallet (a beginner's guide)

A wallet is how you hold crypto. Get the setup right once and you avoid the mistakes that cost beginners the most — here's the safe way.

The big idea

A wallet is not a bank

With a self-custody wallet, you hold the keys. There's no password reset and no one to reverse a mistake — which is exactly why the backup step below matters more than anything else.

Step 1 — Pick the right type

  • Custodial vs self-custody: custodial = the platform holds your keys (convenient); self-custody = you hold them (full control + full responsibility).
  • Hot vs cold: hot wallets are online and handy for small amounts; cold wallets stay offline and are safer for holding.

Step 2 — Install from the official source only

Download a reputable wallet only from its official site or app-store listing. Fake wallet apps and lookalike sites are a common way beginners get drained.

Step 3 — Back up your seed phrase (offline)

  • Write the recovery seed phrase on paper and store it somewhere safe and offline.
  • Never type it into a website, photo, or chat — anyone with it controls your crypto.
  • No legitimate support team will ever ask for it.

Step 4 — Test with a small amount

Before moving anything meaningful, send a small test amount and confirm it arrives. Verify the address and network every time. Transfers can't be undone.

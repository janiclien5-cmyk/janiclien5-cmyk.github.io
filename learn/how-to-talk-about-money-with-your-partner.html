How to talk about money with your partner

Money is one of the most common things couples argue about — and one of the least talked about calmly. A few habits turn it from a fight into a team effort.

The mindset shift

It's a team conversation, not a confrontation

The goal isn't to win — it's to get on the same page. Approach money talks as two people solving a shared problem, not as opponents.

Habits that help

  • Make it regular: a short, calm money chat beats a tense once-a-year showdown.
  • Share goals and worries honestly — and listen to theirs.
  • Agree on the big stuff: how you'll split costs, save, and make large decisions.
  • No blame: meet different money habits with curiosity, not judgment.

When you don't agree

Different upbringings mean different money instincts — that's normal. Find the shared aim underneath (security, freedom, a home), and work from there. Small compromises beat silent resentment.

Curious about crypto?

Decide it together

Anything risky — like crypto — is best discussed openly as a couple, treated as a small amount you can both afford to lose, never shared savings or essentials. If you're curious to learn, start with the basics, safely.

APR vs APY: what's the difference?

These two look almost identical and get mixed up constantly. The difference is small but real — and it comes down to compounding.

The core difference

Compounding is what separates them

APR (annual percentage rate) is the yearly rate without compounding. APY (annual percentage yield) includes compounding, so it reflects what you actually earn or pay over a year. APY is usually a touch higher than the matching APR.

Which to look at

  • Borrowing: APR is commonly quoted as the cost of a loan or card.
  • Saving / investing: APY shows your true return because it counts compounding.

The practical tip

Always compare like for like — APR to APR, or APY to APY. Comparing an APR to an APY makes one option look better than it really is. A small rate difference adds up over time, so it's worth a glance.

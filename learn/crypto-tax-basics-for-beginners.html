Crypto tax basics for beginners

Tax is the part most beginners ignore — until it's stressful. Here are the plain-English basics so it isn't a surprise. (Rules vary a lot by country.)

The honest disclaimer first

Rules vary — check your local ones

Crypto tax depends heavily on where you live. This is general education, not tax advice — for your situation, check your local tax authority or a qualified professional.

Why crypto can be taxable

Many tax authorities treat crypto as property or an asset, not as regular money. That means certain actions can create a taxable event — even if you never cashed out to your bank.

Events that often count (depending on your country)

  • Selling crypto for cash at a gain or loss.
  • Swapping one coin for another — often treated as a sale.
  • Spending crypto on goods or services.
  • Receiving crypto as income or rewards.
  • Just buying and holding is often not taxable — but rules differ.

The one habit that saves you

Keep records from day one: dates, amounts, and values for every transaction. You may need them to work out what (if anything) you owe, and reconstructing them later is painful.

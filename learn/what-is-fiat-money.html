What is fiat money? (and how it compares to crypto)

You'll hear crypto people say "fiat" a lot. It just means regular money — and understanding it makes crypto easier to understand by contrast.

In one line

Government money, not backed by gold

Fiat money is regular government-issued currency — dollars, euros, pounds — that isn't backed by a physical commodity. Its value comes from trust in the government and the fact that everyone accepts it for goods and debts.

How it differs from crypto

  • Control: fiat is issued and managed by central authorities; crypto is decentralized.
  • Supply: fiat can be created by central banks; many cryptos have a fixed cap.
  • Stability: fiat is fairly stable day to day; crypto is far more volatile.
  • Acceptance: fiat is accepted nearly everywhere; crypto far less so.

The honest takeaway

Each has trade-offs — fiat's stability and acceptance vs crypto's decentralization and fixed supply. One isn't simply "better." Understanding both helps you think clearly instead of following hype on either side.

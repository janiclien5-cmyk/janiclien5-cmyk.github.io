How to read a crypto chart (a beginner's guide)

Charts look intimidating, but a few basics cover most of what beginners need — starting with the most important truth about them.

Read this first

A chart shows the past, not the future

Charts describe what already happened. They help you understand context and manage risk — they do not predict the next move. Anyone who says otherwise is guessing.

1. Candlesticks

Each candle shows four prices for a period: the open, high, low, and close. A green/up candle closed higher than it opened; a red/down candle closed lower. The thin "wicks" show the highest and lowest points reached.

2. Trend

The big-picture direction: higher highs and higher lows is an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows is a downtrend; sideways is a range. Zoom out — the trend on a 5-minute chart can look opposite to the one on a daily chart.

3. Support & resistance

Support is a level where price has stopped falling before; resistance is where it has stopped rising. They're areas of interest, not certainties — levels break.

4. Volume

Volume shows how much was traded. A move on high volume has more conviction behind it than the same move on thin volume. It's context, not a signal by itself.

What is inflation? (explained simply)

Inflation is why your grandparents' prices sound made up — and why cash quietly loses power over time. Here's the plain-English version.

In one line

Prices rise, so money buys a little less

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. The same money buys a bit less than it used to. Modest, steady inflation is normal in most economies — the worry is when it gets high or unpredictable.

Why it happens (briefly)

Roughly: when there's more demand than supply, or more money chasing the same goods, prices drift up. Central banks try to keep it low and steady, with mixed success across different times and places.

Why it matters to you

Cash sitting idle slowly loses buying power. $100 under the mattress buys less in ten years than today. That's why people think about saving and investing in ways that aim to at least keep pace with inflation — while remembering every option carries its own risk.

Curious about crypto?

An honest note

You'll hear crypto pitched as an "inflation hedge." The honest truth: it's highly volatile and that role is debated, not proven — it can fall sharply regardless of inflation. If you're curious, learn it carefully as a small, high-risk slice, only with money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

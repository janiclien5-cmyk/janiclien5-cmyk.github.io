What is market cap in crypto?

It's the number that stops you falling for the "this coin is only $0.001, it's cheap!" trap. Here's what market cap means and why it matters more than price.

In one line

Price × coins in circulation

Market cap = the coin's price multiplied by how many coins are currently in circulation. It estimates the total value of all the coins — a far better size measure than the price of one coin.

Why price alone misleads

A coin priced at $0.0001 can have trillions of units, giving it a massive market cap. Another at $50 might have very few. So "cheap" by price tag tells you almost nothing — the market cap and supply do.

A quick example

  • Coin A: $0.001 × 100 billion coins = $100 million market cap.
  • Coin B: $100 × 1 million coins = $100 million market cap.
  • Same total value — wildly different price tags. The price alone fooled you.

How beginners use it

Use market cap to gauge a coin's rough size and compare projects fairly — and remember a big market cap isn't a safety badge either. It's one number among many, not a verdict.

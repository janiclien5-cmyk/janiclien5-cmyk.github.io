How do credit cards work? (a beginner's guide)

Used well, a credit card is a convenient tool. Used badly, it's an expensive trap. The difference comes down to a few simple mechanics.

In one line

Borrow now, repay later — with a catch

A credit card lets you borrow up to a limit, then repay later. Pay the full balance by the due date and you usually owe no interest. Don't, and interest piles onto what's left — and credit-card interest is typically high.

The key terms

  • Credit limit: the most you can borrow.
  • Grace period: the window to pay in full and avoid interest.
  • Minimum payment: the small amount you must pay — paying only this keeps you in debt.
  • APR: the interest rate on any balance you carry.

The one rule that keeps you safe

Treat it like a debit card: only spend what you already have, and pay the full balance every month. Do that and a card is convenient (and can build your credit score); don't, and the interest does the damage.

Curious about crypto?

Clear the card, build the basics first

Never put crypto on credit — high-interest debt is the opposite of building wealth. Clear any balance and get your savings basics in place first; then, if you're curious, learn about crypto carefully, only with money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.

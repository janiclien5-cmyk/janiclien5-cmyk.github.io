What is gas in crypto? (network fees, explained)

Ever sent a tiny amount of crypto and watched a surprise fee eat into it? That's gas. Here's what it is and why it jumps around.

In one line

The fee to process your transaction

Gas is the fee you pay a blockchain to process and confirm your transaction. It pays the network for the computing work — and it's separate from any trading fee an exchange charges.

Why it spikes

Gas works like surge pricing: when lots of people want transactions confirmed at once, they compete for limited space in each block, and fees rise. When the network is quiet, gas drops. The same simple transfer can cost very different amounts depending on the moment.

How to pay less

  • Transact when the network is less busy (often off-peak hours).
  • Avoid lots of small transactions — batch when you can.
  • Some networks have far lower fees than others; check before you send.

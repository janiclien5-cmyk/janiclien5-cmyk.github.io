What is a 51% attack? (crypto security, simply)

It sounds alarming, and on small networks it's a real risk. Here's what a 51% attack is — and why big chains are largely safe from it.

In one line

One party controlling most of the network

A 51% attack is when a single party gains control of more than half of a blockchain's mining or staking power. With that majority, they could rewrite recent transactions or spend the same coins twice (a "double-spend").

Why it matters

A blockchain's security relies on no single party having too much control. A 51% attack breaks that assumption, which can undermine trust in the affected network and the value of its coin.

Why big chains are largely safe

On a large network like Bitcoin, gathering that much power would cost an enormous amount of money and energy — usually far more than any attacker could gain. That difficulty is a core reason big chains are considered secure. Small, low-power networks are far more vulnerable.

The beginner takeaway

You don't need to fear a 51% attack on a major coin — but it's one more reason to be cautious with tiny, obscure projects, where the network may be weak.

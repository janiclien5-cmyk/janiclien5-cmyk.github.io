What is a stablecoin? (a beginner's explainer)

If Bitcoin's price swings scare you, stablecoins are the part of crypto that tries to stay still. Here's how they work — and why "stable" isn't a sure thing.

In one line

A crypto designed to hold a steady value

A stablecoin aims to track a steady value — usually a currency like the US dollar — so it doesn't swing like other crypto. It's used to move value around or sit out volatility without cashing out entirely.

The main types

  • Fiat-backed: backed by reserves of real currency held somewhere — you're trusting that the reserves exist.
  • Crypto-backed: backed by other crypto locked as collateral (usually over-collateralized).
  • Algorithmic: tries to hold the peg with code and incentives, no real backing — historically the riskiest.

The risk: the peg can break

"Stable" is a goal, not a certainty. A stablecoin can lose its peg (a "depeg"), and several have — sometimes badly. The safety depends on what actually backs it, so the peg is a claim to check, not a promise to trust.

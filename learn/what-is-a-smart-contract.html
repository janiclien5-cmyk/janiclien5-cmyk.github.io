What is a smart contract? (for beginners)

It sounds technical, but the idea is simple — and it's behind a huge amount of what you hear about in crypto.

In one line

Code that runs itself on a blockchain

A smart contract is a small program stored on a blockchain that runs automatically when its conditions are met — no person or company needed in the middle.

The vending-machine analogy

Think of a vending machine: put in the right amount, press the button, and it gives you the item — automatically, the same way every time. A smart contract is similar: the right input triggers the defined output, exactly as the code says.

Where they're used

Smart contracts power DeFi (lending, swapping), NFTs, and many apps on networks like Ethereum.

The risk you must know

"Runs exactly as written" cuts both ways: if the code has a bug or is malicious, funds can be drained — and there's usually no undo. Favor well-known, audited projects, and never interact with a contract you don't understand.

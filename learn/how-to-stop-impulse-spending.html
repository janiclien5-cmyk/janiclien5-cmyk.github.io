How to stop impulse spending

Impulse buys are designed to be easy — one tap, instant dopamine. The fix isn't willpower; it's adding a little friction and time.

Why it happens

Emotion + zero friction

Impulse buys are usually driven by a feeling — stress, boredom, excitement — and made effortless by saved cards and one-tap checkouts. The trick is to interrupt that frictionless loop.

Simple tricks that work

  • The 24-hour rule: wait a day before any non-essential buy. Most urges fade.
  • Remove saved cards: if buying takes effort, you'll do it less.
  • Unsubscribe: kill the marketing emails and "sale" notifications.
  • Shop with a list and stick to it.
  • Name the feeling: ask "am I buying this, or fixing a mood?"

Redirect the saved money

Every avoided impulse buy is money freed up. Send it somewhere on purpose — a emergency fund, a sinking fund, or debt — so it actually builds something.

Curious about crypto?

The same discipline applies

FOMO and impulse buys hurt in crypto even more. If you ever explore it, use the same patience — learn first, never rush, and only risk money you can afford to lose. Start with the basics, safely.
